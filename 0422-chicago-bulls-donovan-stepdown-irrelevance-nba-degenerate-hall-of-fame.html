Billy Donovan Quit The Bulls And Nobody Noticed Because The Bulls Are Chicago's Best-Kept Loss

Six seasons of basketball that happened in the same city as Oprah and yet somehow less watched. Wilbon finally said it out loud. My bet slip said it first.

Billy Donovan resigned as head coach of the Chicago Bulls this week and the sports world responded with a collective "oh yeah, him." Six seasons. The man coached the Chicago Bulls for six seasons. Six. That is longer than some marriages. That is longer than my last attempt to quit sports betting. And during those six seasons, the Bulls were so profoundly mediocre that Michael Wilbon went on television and said the quiet part loud, which was that the Bulls are as irrelevant as any big-market team in sports. Michael Wilbon. On air. He said it. And the only people surprised were the people who had not been betting on the Bulls. Those of us who had been betting on the Bulls were stunned, but only because someone finally said out loud what our wallets have been screaming at us since 2017.

I have lost more money on the Chicago Bulls than I have on any team that does not have the word Knicks in its name, and I am proud of exactly zero percent of that sentence. Every year, like a clockwork ritual performed by a drunk man who has misplaced his pants, I look at the preseason NBA lines, see the Bulls win total, and think "that number is too low." Every year I am wrong. Every year the sportsbook smiles a quiet smile and writes my name down in a little book titled Guys Who Keep Coming Back. I am a featured subscriber.

The Six Seasons Of Nothing Happening

Here is the thing about the Donovan era that makes it such a perfect testament to Bulls irrelevance. I cannot, without Googling, remember a single memorable playoff moment from his tenure. There are moments, presumably. There must be. The man coached an NBA team in a major market for six years. Statistically, one of those games must have mattered to somebody. But if you asked a bar full of degenerates to name one Bulls playoff game from the last six seasons, the bar would go silent, someone would eventually say "I think DeMar DeRozan hit a shot one time," and then the conversation would move back to Kevin Durant's calf. Which, by the way, is apparently still barking.

Six seasons. For six seasons, the Bulls played in the United Center in front of people who showed up mostly because Chicago is a sports town and it was something to do on a Tuesday. The Bulls were never bad enough to be interesting. They were never good enough to be scary. They were the sports equivalent of a car that runs fine but you cannot remember the last time you drove it. Meanwhile, in the exact same building, the Blackhawks went through an entire dynasty, dismantled it, rebuilt it, and are now talking about another playoff push, and the Bulls were still there the whole time, hovering around .500, signing DeMar DeRozan to another contract.

The Bets I Made That I Should Have Known Better

In 2022 I took the Bulls at plus three against Milwaukee and wrote "LOCK" next to the entry in my notes. Lock. I used the word lock. The Bulls lost by 18. The handwritten "LOCK" is still there, circled, in permanent ink, in a notebook I cannot throw out because it is my shame museum. In 2023 I played a Bulls team total over. They scored 88 points. 88. A college team would score 88 by accident. In 2024 I parlayed the Bulls moneyline with two other "safe" picks and watched all three lose on the same night while my cat judged me from the other couch.

This is the betting experience of an entire generation of Bulls degens. Not devastating losses. Not agony. Just a thin, consistent bleeding of money that you almost do not notice until you check your bankroll in March and realize the Bulls have been a minus sign in your life for eight straight months. Wilbon is not exaggerating. The Bulls are the quietest way to lose money in American sports, and somehow they keep finding a way to do it in front of a packed arena in the third-largest media market in the country.

The Donovan Farewell Nobody Is Giving Him

Stepping down is supposed to be a moment. Pat Riley got a parade of goodbyes. Popovich still has not fully stepped away and people are writing retrospectives. Billy Donovan quietly informed the Bulls organization that he was done, the news cycled through ESPN for about 47 minutes, Kendrick Perkins said the correct thing on television, and then the entire sports media ecosystem moved on to the NFL draft. I have seen high school coaches get more airtime when they retire. And the thing is, Donovan is not a bad coach. The man won a national championship at Florida. He coached in Oklahoma City during the KD-Westbrook years, back when KD had a calf that functioned. He is a real basketball mind. The Bulls are where real basketball minds go to disappear.

The only people visibly upset about Donovan leaving are the two or three national writers contractually obligated to say nice things about departing NBA coaches, and possibly whoever ghostwrites the team's social media posts, who now has to compose a "thank you for your service" graphic that will get 3,000 likes and be buried forever. Meanwhile, the Bulls front office, which ESPN is now reporting has multiple other vacancies, is somehow being compared to "any big-market team" in terms of irrelevance. Read that sentence twice. A team in the Madonna-Phil-Jackson-Michael-Jordan family has earned a comparison to the historically irrelevant franchises of pro sports. You did that, Chicago front office. You earned this branding.

The Bulls have not been relevant since the year I thought a razor scooter was an acceptable adult transportation choice.

Why I Will Still Bet Them Next Week

And yet. And yet. The preseason lines will come out again in October. I will look at the Bulls win total. I will think, surely, with a fresh coaching search and maybe one new piece, this number is too low. I will place the bet. I will feel briefly good about myself. And then, in a predictable sequence of events spanning about 82 games, I will quietly lose the unit, refuse to acknowledge it, and tell my group chat in March that I never bet the Bulls to begin with. The group chat will laugh. The group chat knows. The group chat has seen every receipt.

This is degeneracy in its purest form. You cannot actually learn. If you could actually learn, you would not be here. You would have a savings account with actual savings in it, a diversified index fund, and a weekend hobby that does not involve watching Malik Beasley jack up a 28-foot three with the shot clock at 19. You would not know what the phrase "Billy Donovan backdoor cover" means. You would not have a pinned tab open to the Bulls injury report. You would be a functional adult. Instead you are here, in this blog post, reading a 900-word rant about a team that hasn't mattered in a decade, and you are nodding. You are absolutely nodding. I hear you nodding through the internet.

So goodbye, Billy. Thank you for the six seasons of ambient losing that financed three sportsbook lobbies. Thank you for never being so bad that the Bulls got a top-three pick, and never being so good that the Bulls made a deep playoff run. You perfected the art of the 42-win season, and we, the degenerates who kept taking the over, have learned absolutely nothing from it. Whoever the Bulls hire next, I will bet on them too. I will tell myself this time is different. It will not be different. It is never different. That is the magic of Bulls basketball. It is always, always, the same.

Now, if you'll excuse me, I have to go lose a two-team teaser on a Wednesday night. The Bulls are not on it, but that is only because they do not play on Wednesday. I checked.
