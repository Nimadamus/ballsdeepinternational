Five Games, Eight Ways To Lose: The Thursday Getaway-Day Gaping

A five-game Thursday card looked too small to hurt me, so I let my guard all the way down, and a 3:45 day game walked through the open door with eight bet slips and a smile

Five Games, Eight Ways To Lose: The Thursday Getaway-Day Gaping

June 25, 2026 | GAPED WING, DEPARTMENT OF SMALL SLATES THAT WENT BIG

There is a lie I tell myself on days like this, and the lie is short, like the slate. Five games. Only five. How much damage can a man do to himself with a five-game Thursday card, I asked, out loud, to no one, in the voice of a person who has clearly never met himself. A big Saturday with fifteen games, that is dangerous, that is a buffet, a man can hurt himself there. But five little games on a getaway Thursday? That is a snack. That is a safe slate. That is a nap with a side of mild action. I believed this completely, right up until the matinee gaped me before I had finished my coffee.

Here is the math that fooled me, and it is the same math that fools me every single time. Five games feels like five chances to be wrong. But I do not bet games, I bet slips, and on a board this thin I found a way to wedge eight positions into five matchups, because when there are not enough games to spread the damage I simply stack the damage instead. Two team-total unders. A game-total under. A pickem dart I had no business touching. And a fistful of chalk so heavy I needed a forklift and a prayer. Eight ways to lose, folded neatly into an afternoon I had described, in writing, as relaxing.

The 3:45 Day Game Is A Mugging In Broad Daylight

It always starts with the matinee. Athletics and Giants, first pitch at a quarter to four in the afternoon, a time of day no responsible adult should be sweating a moneyline, which is precisely why I was. A getaway-day day game is a special kind of trap, because the lineups are half-rested, the bench gets emptied, the starters are on a flight-to-catch leash, and none of it behaves like a normal night game. I laid the chalk anyway, because the better arm was at home, and I told myself this was discipline. It was not discipline. It was a man betting baseball during a lunch hour and calling it a strategy.

The thing about a day game on a small slate is that it sets the tone for the whole evening, and the tone it set was funereal. Lose the matinee and suddenly the four night games are not action, they are a rescue mission, and rescue missions are where bankrolls go to die. I have written before about the exact moment this descent begins, back when I catalogued every injury I was blaming for my losing streak instead of blaming the man choosing the bets. The day game is just that same disease with an earlier start time. By 7 p.m. I was not betting a slate. I was chasing one.

Two Team-Total Unders, A.K.A. Rooting Against Human Beings Having A Good Day

Then come the team-total unders, the bets where you stop rooting for a result and start rooting against an entire lineup of professional athletes being good at their jobs. I had a team total under here and a team total under there, two separate wagers that both amounted to the same desperate prayer: please, gentlemen, do not score. Stay cold. Strand the runner. Pop it up. I sat in my living room actively willing nine grown men to fail at the one thing they are paid millions to do, which is a deeply strange way to spend an evening and an even stranger thing to risk rent on.

Team-total unders are seductive because they feel smart. You are not betting the whole game, you are surgically fading one bat against one good arm, you tell yourself, like a sniper. But a team total is a single bloop double and a two-out walk away from breaking your heart, and on a five-game night where I needed both of mine to cash, the variance was not a sniper rifle, it was a coin flip I had glued to my forehead. One lineup obliged. The other one, naturally, picked tonight to wake up. That is how you go one-for-two on the safe part of a safe slate.

The Pickem Dart And The Forklift Of Chalk

Let us discuss the dart, because every gaping has one. There was a game with a true pickem, even money, a coin flip the market itself could not separate, and a sane man passes a coin flip. I am not that man. I saw plus money on the better team and I tossed half a unit at it like a tip, a fun little garnish on a slate I was already overcooking. The dart, of course, is never the problem. The problem is that the dart is a gateway, a way of telling yourself you are loose and fearless, right before you go lay the kind of juice that turns fearless into homeless.

And lay it I did. Heavy chalk, the minus-one-fifties and minus-one-forties of the world, favorites so steep that a single win pays for a coffee and a single loss pays for nothing and takes the cup too. The whole logic of laying big chalk is that the favorites are supposed to be safe, and on a tiny slate I leaned on that word, safe, the same word I had used about the slate itself, the same word I will use about next Thursday. I have catalogued this exact behavior over in the Degeneracy wing more times than my dignity can absorb, and still I keep stacking three big favorites like a man building a tower out of anvils.

The Verdict Of The Department Of Small Slates That Went Big

Having reviewed the wreckage, this department finds that a five-game Thursday is not safer than a fifteen-game Saturday. It is more dangerous, because it lulls you. A big slate scares you into a little restraint. A small slate whispers that you are basically already done, go ahead, add another leg, the night is short, what could it cost. What it cost was eight positions, a matinee mugging, a team-total heartbreak, a vanity dart, and a tower of chalk that came down in the seventh inning of a game I was only watching because the other four had already left me for dead.

The lesson is the same one it always is, and I will ignore it the same way I always do. The slate was never the danger. I was the danger, and the slate just gave me a small, quiet, well-lit room to do it in. If you got gaped on the same getaway-day card, the Gaped wing is open all night and the chairs are comfortable. If you would rather pretend it was the injury report's fault, the lifers are filing grievances over in Fuck All. Five games. Only five. I will say it again next week, and I will mean it, and it will gape me again. See you at first pitch.
