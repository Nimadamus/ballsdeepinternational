HR Incident Report #2026-0606: Employee Bet The Spurs To Bounce Back While Down 2-0 In The Finals

Filed with Human Resources for the permanent record, against the employee's wishes

Incident Report: Employee Backed A Down-2-0 Team Because They Were "Due"

June 6, 2026

Per Balls Deep International policy, all repeat offenses must be documented even when the offender is too embarrassed to file them himself. The following incident is logged for the permanent record. The employee involved, who is once again me, has requested that this report be sealed on the grounds that it is "too soon." Request denied. It is never too soon. It is, in fact, exactly on time.

INCIDENT TYPE: Voluntary participation in a closeout series, wrong side.
EVENT: 2026 NBA Finals, Game 3.
SERIES STATE: New York Knicks lead San Antonio Spurs, 2-0.
EMPLOYEE POSITION: Spurs to "bounce back."
STATED REASONING: "They're due."
ACTUAL REASONING: None on file.

Let the record reflect the situation the employee chose to walk into. The New York Knicks lead the San Antonio Spurs two games to none in the NBA Finals. They did not back into this lead. They took Game 2 on the road by a single point, 105 to 104, surviving a Spurs comeback that fell one possession short, with Victor Wembanyama himself watching the final look rim out at the buzzer. New York is now riding a 13-game playoff winning streak. Into this, the employee deposited real money on San Antonio, reasoning that a team this good "had to wake up eventually." HR notes that hope is not a handicapping system.

Section A: Sequence Of Events

The wager was not the product of analysis. The employee did not study the Game 2 film, did not consider that the Spurs had already played their best basketball and still lost by a point, did not weigh that the series was now shifting to a deranged Madison Square Garden crowd that has waited a generation for this. He simply saw the number "2-0," decided it felt unsustainable, and backed the team being buried. This is the betting equivalent of seeing a man already losing a fistfight and putting money on him "for the comeback."

The employee delivered his thesis to the office dog, as is tradition. The thesis was: "Nobody gets swept in the Finals, the Spurs are too talented, and a desperate team at home in Game 3 is live." HR points out that Game 3 is in New York, that the employee did not know this, and that the dog left the room midway through the sentence.

Section B: Aggravating Factors

The phrase "they're due" is classified as a severe aggravating factor. A team being down 2-0 is not evidence that they are about to win. It is evidence that they are down 2-0. The employee has confused the gambler's fantasy, the belief that the universe owes a correction, with an actual edge. HR has seen this exact reasoning in the file before, applied to roulette, applied to a parlay, and applied last week to a man who kept ordering the same sandwich expecting it to eventually be good.

FINDING: "They're due" is not analysis. It is a prayer with a dollar amount attached.

Section C: The Employee's Pre-Loaded Excuses, Submitted As Evidence

Before the opening tip, the employee has already drafted his post-game statements, which we preserve here for completeness:

If the Knicks win and go up 3-0, the employee will claim he was "buying low on a bounce-back spot" and that "the value was in the desperation." If the Spurs somehow steal Game 3, he will claim he "saw it coming a mile away" and that "champions are made in elimination spots," ignoring that the Spurs are not yet in an elimination spot and that he would have to invent one to be right. HR finds both statements to be fan fiction. The honest entry is that the employee bet against the best team in the building because he found their dominance annoying.

Section D: Root Cause Analysis

The pattern is the same as every prior filing. The employee does not bet what is happening. He bets what he feels should happen, then constructs a narrative in which his feeling was wisdom all along. A 2-0 lead built on a one-point road win and a 13-game streak is not a soft spot. It is a flashing sign that says the favorite is favored for excellent reasons. Fading it because it "has to end sometime" is not contrarian genius. It is the employee mistaking his own impatience for insight.

Section E: Recommended Corrective Action (To Be Ignored)

HR recommends, knowing it will be ignored in full: stop betting on teams to do things purely because they have not done them yet. Find out which city the game is in before forming a thesis about home-court desperation. Accept that a 2-0 series lead is information, not an invitation. And retire the phrase "they're due," which has cost the employee more money than any single bad team ever has.

The employee has reviewed these recommendations and declined them. He remains locked on the down-2-0 Spurs to bounce back and intends to suffer through every minute of a Game 3 he bet on a hunch and a grudge. This concludes incident report #2026-0606.
