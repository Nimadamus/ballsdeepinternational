Why I Bet the New England Patriots and Now I'm Questioning Every Life Choice I've Ever Made

A postmortem written at 2 a.m. with a lukewarm light beer, a receipt I refuse to throw away, and the faint sound of Stephen A. Smith still screaming about Kevin Durant somewhere in the background.

The final score was New England 13, Seattle 29, and my soul was somewhere between the two. I backed the Patriots. I looked at a home line that felt generous, nodded at my monitor like a man who knows things, and clicked confirm with the same calm energy as a man lighting a bonfire inside a fireworks warehouse. The Seahawks showed up in Foxborough, scored almost 30 points, and handed New England a loss so thorough it felt personally targeted. I do not even live in Massachusetts. It still felt targeted.

This is not the first time I have bet on the Patriots under the working theory that "they are due." They are not due. They have been "not due" since the day Tom Brady left for Florida. I know this. Every Sunday I know this. And yet every Sunday my little goblin brain types P-A-T-R-I-O-T-S into the betslip and hovers over the confirm button like it is going to send a kidney to an ex-girlfriend. Click. Loss. Shame. Repeat.

The Five Stages of Losing a Patriots Bet

Denial arrives first. When Seattle scored 14 early, I told myself it was just an ugly start, New England was clearly about to flip a switch, any second now, the switch was coming. There was no switch. There was never a switch. There has not been a switch in New England since a quarterback named Mac Jones was briefly fooled into thinking he would have a career.

Anger shows up around halftime. This is when I turned to my couch cushion and said things to it that I would not repeat to my own mother. The couch cushion has heard worse. The couch cushion is a hardened veteran of my betting life and has absorbed enough of my profanity to qualify for PTSD benefits. When I yelled at the head coach through a television that was not connected to the head coach in any way, I felt briefly better, then immediately worse. This is bargaining. You wake up from bargaining by being down 23-7 with 9 minutes left in the third.

Depression is me opening the fridge for no reason. The fridge contains a condiment bottle from 2019 and half a lime. The lime has a hat of mold. I close the fridge. I open it again. The lime has not changed. Neither has the score. Acceptance is the fourth stage and I have never reached it. I have never accepted a Patriots loss in my life. My heart is a museum of Patriots losses displayed in shadow boxes with little plaques.

KD Is Hurt and Apparently That's My Problem Too

While I was watching my NFL ticket catch fire, Stephen A. Smith was on ESPN explaining to me that the Houston Rockets had no chance without Kevin Durant against the Lakers. I had not bet on the Rockets. I had not even been thinking about the Rockets. But Stephen A. yelled KEVIN DURANT at me so many times that I felt personally responsible for the Rockets losing a playoff game I was not financially involved in. That is the power of ESPN. It can make you feel bad about a game you did not bet on while you are still losing a different game you did bet on.

This is also, I should note, the Kevin Durant excuse hall of fame. Any time a team you backed loses in the NBA playoffs, there is a 90 percent chance you will now blame Kevin Durant's hamstring, knee, calf, or the mere concept of Kevin Durant's body. I have blamed Kevin Durant's body for losses in fantasy football. I have blamed Kevin Durant's body for a parlay that did not include a basketball game. The man is a walking alibi for my entire betting history, and at this point it would be irresponsible not to just preemptively blame him for everything going forward.

The Bulls Are Hiring and So Is My Wife If She Finds Out About This Week

Between rounds of hating myself, ESPN also told me the Bulls got permission to interview candidates for their front office vacancies. I too should be hiring candidates for vacancies. My vacancy is the job of "person who tells me to stop betting on the Patriots." The job description is simple. Smack the phone out of my hand on Sundays. Benefits include a clean conscience and possibly seeing my money again.

I also learned that Trent Williams signed a two-year, 50 million dollar extension with the 49ers. Trent Williams is going to make more money in the next 24 months than I will see in my entire life. Trent Williams is not questioning his life choices right now. Trent Williams is at a brunch. I am at a funeral, and the deceased is my unit size.

The worst part of losing a Patriots bet is not the money. It is the fact that my group chat is already typing.

What I Learned (And Will Immediately Ignore)

The lesson is always the same. Stop backing bad teams just because the line feels good. Bad teams have bad lines because they are bad. The sportsbook knows this. The sportsbook knows this so well that they employ entire buildings of people whose only job is to know this. I, on the other hand, have a Wednesday afternoon and a vague feeling about home-field advantage.

I will, of course, bet on the Patriots again next week. I will tell you I will not. I will tell myself I will not. My hand will betray me. I will be back here writing another one of these. You will read it. We will pretend together that this is fine. It is not fine. But it is also not quite not-fine either. It is just Sunday.

Goodnight. The lime is still moldy. The Seahawks covered. Kevin Durant's hamstring is tighter than my bankroll. May your degenerate weekend be slightly less embarrassing than mine.
