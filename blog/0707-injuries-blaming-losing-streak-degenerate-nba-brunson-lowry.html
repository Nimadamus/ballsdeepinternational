Injuries I Am Blaming For My Losing Streak: A Degenerate's Comprehensive List, Featuring Jalen Brunson's Wrist And Kyle Lowry's One-Day Retirement

Posted July 7, 2026 · Filed under Degeneracy · Offseason Sweat Desk

Every degenerate reaches a point in a losing streak where he stops examining his own decisions and starts examining the medical reports of professional athletes he has never met. This is a survival instinct. If I am the reason my parlays keep dying, then I have to change, and change is hard and requires therapy and a budgeting app. But if the reason is a stranger's ligament, then I am blameless, a victim of circumstance, a good handicapper cursed by the injury gods. So in the interest of my own mental health and absolutely nothing else, I have assembled the comprehensive, medically irresponsible list of injuries I am personally blaming for the current state of my bankroll.

Exhibit A: Jalen Brunson's Left Wrist

The New York Knicks announced that Jalen Brunson underwent surgery on his left wrist, with a recovery timeline of roughly two months before he returns to basketball activities. Two months. Do you understand what that does to a man who has already emotionally pre-loaded his entire next-season futures card around this guy? Brunson, by the way, reportedly played through the discomfort in his shooting arm during the postseason, which is the sort of tough-guy detail that sportswriters love and that I have chosen to interpret as a direct personal attack. He gets to be a warrior. I get to be a guy explaining to a customer service bot why a bet from March still feels unresolved in my heart.

Here is the thing about a July wrist surgery: there is no game to bet. The injury does not settle anything. It just sits there, glowing on the futures board four months before the season starts, daring me to adjust a win total based on a wrist that will be perfectly fine by training camp. And I will. I will move a number in my head because of a July medical update, which is the exact behavior of a person who should not be allowed near an app.

An offseason injury report is a rumor with an MRI attached. You are not betting on a wrist. You are betting on your own inability to leave a healthy man alone for eight weeks.

Exhibit B: Kyle Lowry Retiring, Which I Am Counting As An Injury

On July 7, Kyle Lowry signed a one-day contract with the Toronto Raptors so he could officially retire as a member of the franchise, the same organization he helped carry to a championship in 2019. The Raptors are retiring his number 7, and the whole thing was staged on the seventh day of the seventh month to match the jersey. It is genuinely beautiful. Forty years old, twenty seasons, a legend closing the loop in the city that loved him. A grown adult with a functioning emotional life would tear up and move on.

I am not that adult. I have filed Lowry's retirement under Injuries, because as far as my parlay history is concerned, every Lowry playoff run he ever went on took something from me, and now that he is walking away for good I have to accept that I never once beat the man. He retires clean. I retire nothing but hope. A one-day contract is the classiest exit in sports, and I have converted it into a personal grievance, which tells you everything you need to know about the condition of the patient writing this.

Exhibit C Through Exhibit Infinity: Everything Else

Once you start blaming injuries, you cannot stop, because the human body is a betrayal machine and the injury report never sleeps. A day-to-day tag on a role player I forgot I had in a same-game parlay. A load-management note dropped ninety minutes before tip. A star who gets ruled out after I have already talked myself into the over. Every one of these gets added to the list, cross-referenced against my losses, and used as evidence in the ongoing trial of Me Versus Accountability, a case that has never once reached a verdict.

The uncomfortable truth, the one I bury under the list, is that the injuries are not the problem. I bet the same way when everyone is healthy. I chased the Wiggins signing when there were no games at all, which I confessed when the Heat handed Andrew Wiggins sixty-four million dollars and I still could not get a twenty-dollar parlay refunded. I negotiated with the sky itself when a rain delay left my bet slip frozen in purgatory. Injuries did not do that. I did that. The wrist is innocent. Lowry is innocent. The tarp was innocent.

The Verdict, Delivered To Myself, By Myself, Ignored By Myself

So here is the honest medical summary. Jalen Brunson will be back by camp and his wrist owes me nothing. Kyle Lowry earned the cleanest retirement in the sport and my losing tickets are not his responsibility. The day-to-day guys are day-to-day. The load management is load management. None of it is a conspiracy against my bankroll, because the universe is not organized around ruining my specific parlays, no matter how personally it feels at 11:40 on a Tuesday.

The move, the actual disciplined move, is to close the injury report, stop assigning blame to strangers' ligaments, and admit that a losing streak is usually just a series of bets a person should not have made. I know this. I have written it down. I have read it back to myself. And now, having achieved total clarity and emotional maturity, I am going to go check whether Brunson's wrist has moved his win total even a little, purely for research, like a man who has learned absolutely nothing and intends to keep it that way.
