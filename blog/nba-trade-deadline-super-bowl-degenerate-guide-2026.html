The NBA Trade Deadline: Where Your Futures Bets Go to Die

If you had Cavaliers futures tickets sitting pretty, congratulations and also my condolences, because Cleveland just traded Darius Garland to the Clippers for 36-year-old James Harden and a second-round pick. That's right. The Cavaliers looked at their 30-21 record with Donovan Mitchell humming along and said, "You know what this team needs? James Harden. The guy who has demanded a trade from roughly 40% of all NBA franchises."

Now look, Harden is averaging 25.4 points and 8.1 assists through 44 games this season. Those are genuine numbers. The man can still cook. But pairing him with Mitchell means Cleveland now has two ball-dominant guards who both want the rock in crunch time, and if you've ever watched Harden in a playoff fourth quarter, well, let's just say the strip clubs near Rocket Mortgage FieldHouse are about to see a lot more activity.

Meanwhile, the Clippers get Garland, who is 26 years old and has been limited to just 26 games this season because of separate toe injuries. So the Clippers essentially traded a 36-year-old who's playing great right now for a 26-year-old who can't stay on the court. This is what we in the degenerate community call a "both teams lose" trade, which means the real winners are people who bet on chaos.

The Ringer called Harden "trapped in Groundhog Day" and honestly that's also what my betting account looks like every morning.

Anthony Davis to the... Wizards?

If you thought the Harden trade was wild, the Mavericks shipped Anthony Davis to Washington in an EIGHT-PLAYER blockbuster. The Mavs sent Davis, Jaden Hardy, D'Angelo Russell, and Dante Exum to the Wizards and got back Khris Middleton, AJ Johnson, Malaki Branham, Marvin Bagley III, two firsts, and three seconds.

Dallas is now building around rookie Cooper Flagg after already trading Luka Doncic to the Lakers last year, which means the Mavericks front office has essentially turned a championship contender into a D-League roster in twelve months. And somehow, the Wizards now have Anthony Davis, a man who gets injured walking to the team bus. Washington is going to spend the next three months bubble-wrapping this guy like he's a porcelain vase being shipped across the country.

Oh, and Davis has a hand injury that's expected to sideline him for most of February. The Wizards traded for a guy who's currently injured. In February. During a season. I cannot make this up.

The Giannis Watch: Much Ado About Absolutely Nothing

Every NBA insider on the planet spent the last two weeks breathlessly reporting that Giannis Antetokounmpo was "available" and that the Timberwolves, Heat, Knicks, and Warriors were all in the mix. The Warriors went so far as to offer Draymond Green, Brandin Podziemski, Jonathan Kuminga, and multiple draft picks. Milwaukee said no. Minnesota made their pitch. Milwaukee said no. Miami called. Milwaukee said no.

So Giannis is staying in Milwaukee, which means everyone who adjusted their futures bets based on "Giannis to Golden State" rumors just paid a stupidity tax. The Warriors, unable to land the Greek Freak, pivoted to trading Kuminga and Buddy Hield to the Hawks for Kristaps Porzingis. Jimmy Butler is already there after being traded from the Heat, and now Steph Curry has Porzingis, a 7-foot-3 unicorn who is also made of glass. The Warriors' medical staff is going to need a raise.
