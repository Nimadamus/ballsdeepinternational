Why I Bet on the Patriots and the Seahawks Personally Broke My Spirit

April 25, 2026, 10:02 PM

The final score was Seahawks 29, Patriots 13. I had New England minus the points like a man who has never been hurt before. The Seahawks did not consult me. The Seahawks did not return my calls. The Seahawks showed up in Foxborough on a Sunday afternoon, ate a piece of New England's soul on the field, took a long look at the scoreboard, and decided 16 was not a wide enough margin and pressed the ball downfield until it was. I sat on my couch in my lucky team jersey and watched my parlay leg go up in flames in real time, like a degenerate cremating his own Sunday over a four-team teaser that ate at the Patriots' line.

This is not a hot take. This is a hospice eulogy. I am writing this from the floor of my living room, surrounded by takeout containers, with my shame next to me wearing a foam finger.

I Knew. I Did It Anyway. That Is the Whole Disease.

The lookahead spot was right there. The injury report was right there. The fact that the Patriots' offensive line is held together with electrical tape and a single fervent prayer from Robert Kraft was right there. I read it. I clocked it. Then I told myself, the way every degenerate tells himself, that I had a feel for this one. The Seahawks are a road team out of the time zone, the public is on Seattle, and the fade is the move. That is what I told myself. That is what every losing parlay ticket starts with. I had a feel for this one.

I had no feel for this one. The only thing I had a feel for this one was the texture of my couch as I sank further into it.

The Defense Showed Up Wearing the Wrong Uniform

You know what New England's defense did against the Seahawks. They did the thing where they let the running back run sideline to sideline like he was on a moving walkway in an airport. They missed the kind of tackles that pee-wee coaches get fired for. They blew coverage on a wheel route that, I want to be very specific here, every middle school defensive coordinator in America has practiced defending. The wheel route. The Patriots got cooked by a wheel route. In Foxborough. In April. With the season already over for them in any meaningful sense. With my money on the line.

I am not saying the Seahawks are unbeatable. I am saying that on this particular Sunday, the Patriots could have been replaced on the field by a high school team and the only thing that would have changed is the over would have hit harder.

The Quarterback Situation Is Held Together With Tape and Hope

I do not need to say his name. You know who. He took a sack on third down with eighteen seconds on the play clock. Eighteen. Seconds. Eighteen seconds is the amount of time it takes to throw the ball away, fake a slide, scramble, audible into a different play, do a little dance, and then throw the ball away again. He chose, instead, to be tackled in his own end zone, on his own field, in front of his own crowd, by a defensive end who started the morning eating a hotel waffle. The safety was the dagger. Two points. That two points hit my teaser like a brick through a plate-glass window.

Why I Will Bet on the Patriots Again Next Week

Because I have a sickness. Because the line will move and the value will look right and the public will be off them and I will read three articles about how this is a get-right spot and I will drive back to the same window I just got eviscerated at and I will hand the same teller my same money and ask, in a voice that has lost all dignity, can I have a teaser please. The teller will not make eye contact. The teller has seen this movie. The teller knows how it ends. The teller takes the money anyway, because the teller is also held together with electrical tape and a single fervent prayer.

The Patriots are not a football team to me. They are a relationship I cannot leave. We have been through too much. I have receipts in my wallet from 2019 with their logo on them. I have a Mac Jones rookie card I bought ironically that I have since hidden in shame. I have a sweatshirt that no longer fits. I am not going to throw any of it away. I am going to keep it, like all the artifacts of a long emotional misadventure, because if I throw any of it out it means it was all for nothing. And if it was all for nothing, then I have to look at the spreadsheet.

The Spreadsheet

Do not ask about the spreadsheet. The spreadsheet is private. The spreadsheet is between me and my god. The spreadsheet has a column for Patriots picks ATS and another column for Patriots picks ML and another column for Patriots picks against the spread in road divisional games and the bottom of the spreadsheet has a number that I do not look at, ever, under any circumstances, even when sober. I keep the spreadsheet in a folder labeled "Tax Documents" because if my wife finds it she will have grounds for an annulment.

The Seahawks Did This on Purpose

The Seahawks knew. They knew I had this game. They knew I had eight units on it. They knew I had it in a teaser. They knew I had it in a parlay. They knew I had a side prop on the running back going under his rushing yards because I, a fool with a phone, had read a fantasy football article that morning. The Seahawks held a team meeting on Saturday night and they watched film of my betting account. They studied my tendencies. They game-planned for the destruction of my Sunday specifically. And then they came out and ran the gameplan, perfectly, on national television, while I sat on my couch and slowly came to understand that the Patriots are not a team I bet on, the Patriots are a slot machine that exclusively pays out in heartbreak.

Final Tally for the Sunday Window

  • Patriots minus the points: lost.
  • Patriots moneyline parlay leg: lost.
  • Under 47.5 in the Pats game: lost, because of course it lost, the Seahawks dropped 29.
  • Same-game parlay involving Pats receiver over 4.5 receptions: lost, because the receiver was targeted twice all afternoon and one of them was a screen pass that lost yards.
  • My emotional baseline: lost.

The point of the Sunday window is to bet less than you can afford and feel a little bit of something. I bet more than I could afford and I felt all of it. The Seahawks did this to me. Bill O'Brien did this to me. The wheel route did this to me. The Patriots offensive line did this to me. The Robert Kraft luxury suite did this to me. They all did. And I will not learn. I will be back next week. The window opens at noon. So do I.
