Debauchery: True Stories of Wild Nights and Regrettable Decisions

Wild nights, regrettable decisions, and stories that start with "So anyway..."

Welcome to the Debauchery Archives

Every civilization has its historians. The Greeks had Herodotus. The Romans had Tacitus. And now, in the grand tradition of documenting human behavior at its most spectacularly unhinged, Balls Deep International has us. Welcome to Debauchery, the section of this site dedicated entirely to the stories that start with an open bar, a bad idea, or a flight to a country where the legal drinking age is "can you reach the counter," and end with someone explaining to a hotel manager, a law enforcement officer, or a disappointed family member exactly how things got this out of hand.

These are not hypothetical scenarios. These are not creative writing exercises. Every story on this page is rooted in the real, messy, occasionally illegal, and always deeply regrettable experiences of actual human beings who, for reasons that remain unclear even to them, decided that the appropriate response to "should we do this?" was "absolutely, and let's also do that other thing nobody mentioned."

What Counts as Debauchery?

Glad you asked, because the line is blurrier than your vision at 2 AM after seven drinks you didn't need. Debauchery, as defined by this section, includes but is not limited to: office parties that require new corporate policies to be written; bachelor parties where the groom goes missing for several hours and is later discovered performing unauthorized employment at a casino; wedding receptions that end in ejection, public speaking incidents, and stale dinner rolls; and international trips to cities known for two things, neither of which involves museums, regardless of what you told your mother.

If you've ever woken up in a city you didn't fall asleep in, if you've ever been asked to leave an establishment and briefly considered negotiating, if you've ever ruined a formal event simply by being yourself at full volume, these stories are for you. Not because they'll teach you anything. They won't. Nobody learns from debauchery. That's sort of the defining characteristic.

Why We Document This

Because shame fades, but a good story is forever. Every cautionary tale on this page started as somebody's worst night and became their best anecdote. We believe in the transformative power of sharing your most embarrassing moments with strangers on the internet, not because it heals anything, but because it's funny, and funny is the only currency that never depreciates.

If you're here from our Degeneracy section , you already understand the general vibe. The difference is subtle: degeneracy involves money and gambling. Debauchery involves everything else. The overlap is significant. Sometimes the same night qualifies for both categories. Those are the nights that end up on our Gaped page , where the damage is so complete it deserves its own classification.

A Note on Anonymity and Accuracy

Some names in these stories have been changed. Others have been erased entirely, either out of legal caution or because the person involved has politely requested to be forgotten, which we respect, mostly. The events themselves are as accurate as the memory of someone who was, by definition, not making great choices at the time. We stand by the emotional truth, even if the specific details of "how we ended up in Rotterdam" remain permanently unresolved.

Read on. Judge freely. And if you have a debauchery story of your own that needs a home, know that this section is always growing. Because people never stop making terrible decisions at parties, in foreign countries, and anywhere alcohol is served without proper supervision. It's the one constant in an uncertain world, and we wouldn't have it any other way. Now, if you need a break from wild nights and want some nihilistic rants about modern life , we've got those too.

I Built a Three-Leg Lock Across Two Ballparks and a World Cup Quarterfinal and Called It Free Money, Which Is How I Know I Am Doomed

July 9, 2026

There is a word we degenerates are physically incapable of not saying, and that word is lock. We know it is cursed. We know it has never once been true in the history of organized betting. And yet, on the morning of July 9, I stood in my kitchen with a coffee going cold in my hand and looked at the day's card the way a starving man looks at a vending machine, and I said the word out loud to no one. Lock. Three legs. Free money. I might as well have walked outside and dared the sky to strike me.

Here is how the sickness works. You do not set out to build a parlay. You set out to make one small, responsible, single bet, and then the slate whispers to you. First it showed me the Tigers hosting the Athletics at Comerica, and it showed me the pitching line, and my brain did the thing brains like mine do. Framber Valdez on the mound with a 4.29 ERA against Jack Perkins and his 6.75 ERA. I looked at those two numbers next to each other and I heard a choir. A 6.75 against a 4.29 is not a matchup, I told myself, it is a mismatch, it is a gift, it is the universe finally apologizing for June. That was leg one, and I was already gone.

The Second Leg That Talked Me Into It

A sane man stops at one. I am not a sane man. I scrolled two inches down and there were the Marlins hosting the Mariners, and there was Bryce Miller warming up for Seattle carrying a 1.71 ERA into a ballpark where Miami's bats go to take a nap. So I took the under, or the Mariners, or some flavor of Seattle, because a man with a 1.71 ERA is not a bet, he is a weather forecast, and the forecast was cold and quiet and Miami-shaped.

A 6.75 ERA on one screen, a 1.71 on another. I did not see two baseball games. I saw a staircase to heaven with two steps already built, and I went looking for the third.

The Third Leg, Or How I Laid the Juice

And then, because two legs is for cowards and three legs is for men who deserve what happens to them, I went to the World Cup. France versus Morocco, a quarterfinal, France a heavy favorite. Heavy. The kind of favorite where you have to lay a price so ugly you cannot look directly at it. To win a normal amount of money on France I had to risk a deeply unreasonable amount, the sportsbook equivalent of paying full sticker price and then tipping the dealer. So there it was. Valdez over Perkins. Miller shutting down Miami. France handling Morocco. Three legs, three things I had decided were as certain as sunrise, stapled together into one ticket, one payout, one beautiful stupid number at the bottom that I had already spent in my head on a bass boat I will never own.

Why the Lock Is Never a Lock

Here is the part where I remind you, and myself, that a 6.75 ERA has a funny way of throwing seven shutout innings on the exact night you need him to throw batting practice. A 1.71 ERA has a way of running into the one afternoon the Marlins remember they own bats. And a heavy favorite in a World Cup quarterfinal has a way of hitting a post, watching a goalkeeper stand on his head, and walking off the pitch scoreless while a nation you cannot find on a map celebrates and your ticket turns to ash. The parlay is a chain, and a chain is only as strong as the leg that decides, purely to spite you, to become the story of the tournament.

I know all of this. I have known it for years. It is written on my heart in the ink of a hundred dead tickets. And it does not matter, because tomorrow the slate will whisper again, and there will be another mismatch, another ace, another favorite too heavy to resist, and I will stand in my kitchen with cold coffee and say the word. Lock. That is the whole disease in a single syllable. If you need me, I will be watching three games at once on three devices, praying to a starting pitcher, a Frenchman, and a bullpen that has never once heard my name.
