I Bet My Rent on the Patriots and Now I'm Applying to Be a Seahawks Mascot: A 29-13 Funeral for My Dignity, My Bankroll, and My Will to Live

March 24, 2026

I would like to begin by saying that I am not okay. I am the opposite of okay. I am the antonym of okay in every language, including ones that haven't been invented yet. I am writing this from a Dunkin' Donuts parking lot because I can no longer afford internet at home after the New England Patriots, a franchise that I trusted with my financial future like a man handing his life savings to a raccoon in a business suit, got absolutely eviscerated by the Seattle Seahawks 29-13 on national television. Twenty-nine to thirteen. That's not a football score. That's the ratio of bad decisions I've made this month versus the number of functioning brain cells I have left.

Let me paint you a picture of the man I was before kickoff. Confident. Radiant. Cosmically delusional. Wearing a Tom Brady jersey despite Tom Brady having been retired for years and having nothing to do with this current dumpster fire of a roster. I looked at the Patriots, a team with fewer highlights than a funeral home's Instagram account, and I said, "This is the game they turn it around." I said this out loud. To my cat. My cat walked away, knocked a glass off the counter, and stared at me with the quiet contempt of a being who has never once placed a three-leg parlay. My cat was the smartest creature in that apartment by a margin wider than the final score.

The Pregame Delusion: A Study in Willful Blindness

Here's the thing about being a degenerate sports bettor: you don't learn. You CAN'T learn. Learning would require the part of your brain responsible for pattern recognition to not be completely carbonized from years of believing that "due" is a real statistical concept. The Patriots were due. That's what I told myself. Due for what? Due for a win? Due for basic competence? Due for not making me want to throw my television off the balcony and then follow it? Apparently they were due for none of those things. They were due for a 16-point public execution administered by Seattle with the cold efficiency of a surgeon removing a tumor, except in this metaphor I am the tumor, the tumor is my bankroll, and the surgeon is laughing.

I had a three-leg parlay. Patriots moneyline at +135. Patriots to cover the spread at +3.5. And the under 44.5 total. I hit zero of these. Zero legs. A three-leg parlay where all three legs spontaneously combusted before halftime. That's a batting average of .000, which coincidentally is also my current net worth expressed as a decimal. The parlay was supposed to pay out $487. Instead it paid out a valuable life lesson that I will absolutely ignore by next Sunday.

The First Quarter: Hope is a Disease and I Am Terminal

The Patriots actually looked semi-competent for about six minutes. Six beautiful, lying, treacherous minutes where I thought, "See? This is why I bet on them. I'm a genius. I should start a betting podcast." I was texting my buddy Greg. "Easy money bro," I wrote, with the confidence of a man who has never once made easy money in his entire miserable life. I followed up with "Patriots by 10, book it" and a muscle-arm emoji. Greg didn't respond. Greg stopped responding to my game-day texts sometime around Week 4 after I sent him 47 consecutive messages about a bad beat against the Dolphins, including a voice memo where I was audibly crying. Greg is wise. I am not Greg. I am the cautionary tale that Greg tells other people about at parties.

Then Seattle scored. And scored again. And then scored some more while New England's defense stood around like they'd accidentally wandered onto the field during a stadium tour and were too polite to leave. The Seahawks put up 29 points like it was a scrimmage against a Pop Warner team, and honestly, that's an insult to Pop Warner. At least those kids have the excuse of being eight years old. These are grown men being paid millions of dollars to be this bad at their jobs, and I, a person who can barely afford name-brand cereal, gave them my money voluntarily.

The Halftime Pivot: When Degeneracy Meets Desperation

At halftime, down 20-7, a normal person would have accepted the loss, closed the app, and maybe gone for a walk or read a book or engaged in literally any activity that doesn't involve lighting money on fire and calling it entertainment. I am not a normal person. I am a person who, upon seeing their ship sink, decides the correct course of action is to drill more holes in the hull and then bet on the hull.

So I live-bet the Patriots. Patriots +10.5 second half at -110. "They'll come back," I whispered to nobody, because even my cat had abandoned me at this point, presumably to find an owner with better financial instincts. Possibly a hamster. Then, because I am apparently in a committed relationship with financial ruin, I also took Patriots second-half moneyline at +320, which is the kind of bet you only make when you've confused optimism with a personality disorder. And THEN, because rock bottom apparently has a basement, I hammered a live over on the total, figuring that if the Seahawks were going to score at will, surely SOMEONE on the Patriots would accidentally stumble into the end zone.

They did not come back. They came back like a boomerang that someone threw directly into a wood chipper and then set the wood chipper on fire. Thirteen points. Total. For the entire game. My high school's JV team scored 13 points once, and we went 1-9 that season and our quarterback transferred to another school mid-year because even he couldn't take the embarrassment. At least he had the self-awareness to leave. The Patriots just kept standing there, losing, on television, with my money.
