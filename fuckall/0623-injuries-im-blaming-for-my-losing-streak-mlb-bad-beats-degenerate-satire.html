The Injuries I Am Blaming For My Losing Streak: A Comprehensive List

The Injuries I Am Blaming For My Losing Streak: A Comprehensive List

June 23, 2026 | FUCK ALL WING, OFFICE OF EXCUSES AND DENIAL

I want to be clear about something before we begin. I am not on a losing streak because I am bad at this. I am on a losing streak because the entire injury report of Major League Baseball has organized itself, specifically and with malice, against the contents of my Tuesday bet slip. This is not a confession. This is a grievance, filed formally, with exhibits. Below is the complete and itemized list of every injury, return, rehab start, and pitch-count leash I am holding personally responsible for the state of my bankroll, and I would like it entered into the permanent record that not one single line of it has anything to do with my own decisions.

The losing streak is real. I will give you that much. But a streak needs a cause, and a man cannot survive believing the cause is himself, so I have spent the morning building a more comfortable theory. The theory is that pitching is fake, health is a conspiracy, and every starter in the league is on a secret schedule designed to return from the injured list on precisely the day I bet against the team that needed them healthy. Let us proceed through the evidence.

Exhibit A: The Bieber Comeback That Ruined My Under

Tonight in Toronto, Shane Bieber walks back onto a major league mound for the first time in ages, fresh off an elbow injury, in front of a Blue Jays crowd that has been waiting for him like a returning war hero. And I, a man of discipline, looked at that situation and thought: a guy coming off a long layoff, on a leash, throwing to a bullpen that has to cover innings, that is an under all day. The number begged me. I obliged.

Here is the problem with betting a rehab start. You have no idea which version shows up. Maybe he is rusty and gets pulled after three and the bullpen leaks like a colander. Or maybe the adrenaline of the comeback turns him into prime Bieber for five untouchable innings while Peter Lambert matches him pitch for pitch and the whole thing stays painfully, mockingly low or breaks the wrong way at the exact moment my position needs it not to. An elbow I have never met, attached to a man I have never spoken to, is currently the single largest stakeholder in my financial future. I find that an unacceptable arrangement, and I am blaming the elbow.

Exhibit B: The Woodruff Leash, A Crime Against Pre-Tuesday Me

The carnage did not start tonight. It started yesterday and the day before, when Brandon Woodruff returned for the Brewers off his own long absence, and I confidently treated a returning starter on a hard pitch count like a known quantity. He is not a known quantity. Nobody coming off two months away is a known quantity. A pitch-count leash is the most quietly devastating force in baseball betting because it does not show up on any line. The book knows about the leash. You feel smart for spotting the leash. And then the leash either holds the game down exactly as planned, which somehow still costs you, or the bullpen behind the leash detonates and you learn the leash was a trap baited with your own cleverness.

This is the same disease I diagnosed in myself when I wrote about my sportsbook app sending me more notifications than my own mother, except instead of the app reaching into my brain, it is the entire concept of a starting pitcher's health reaching into my wallet. I cannot turn the injury report off. I have tried. It keeps inventing new comebacks to reach me.

Exhibit C: Every Starter Who Was Mysteriously Healthy When I Needed Them Hurt

Now we arrive at the flip side of the conspiracy, which is the aces who refused to get hurt. I had a position that needed a struggling lineup to keep struggling, and instead they ran into exactly the kind of mediocre arm a normal team scores on, except mine forgot how to hit on the one night it mattered. I had a favorite I laid juice on because the other team's starter has an ERA that belongs in a slow-pitch league, and the slow-pitch arm threw the game of his life. Health, when I need a team weakened, is suddenly robust. Fragility, when I need a team intact, is suddenly everywhere. The injury report is not random. It is a personalized weapon.

I documented the early stages of this descent in my field notes on an entire June of MLB bad beats, but I underestimated how comprehensive the betrayal would become. Back then I thought it was one prop, one hitter, one cruel night. I see now that it was the opening move in a season-long campaign run jointly by the medical staff of all thirty teams.

Exhibit D: The Bullpen, A Hospital Wing Disguised As A Pitching Staff

Let us not forget the relievers, the great unaccountable middle of every baseball bet. You can handicap a starter. You cannot handicap a seventh-inning reliever you have never heard of who got called up from Triple-A on Thursday because three guys ahead of him are on the injured list. Every bullpen in June is a MASH unit, a rotating cast of arms held together with athletic tape and optimism, and they enter the game in the precise inning where my position is either clinging to life or finally, briefly, ahead. They are the variable no model can tame, and they have personally ended more of my evenings than I am prepared to count in a public document.

The Verdict Of The Office Of Excuses And Denial

Having reviewed the evidence, this office finds that the losing streak is, beyond any reasonable doubt, the fault of elbows, hamstrings, rehab assignments, pitch-count leashes, suspiciously healthy aces, and a leaguewide bullpen injury crisis. The bettor is exonerated. The bettor has always been exonerated. The bettor will be exonerated again tomorrow, when there is another card, and another injury report, and another comeback start glowing on the board like a personal insult.

Of course, somewhere underneath all of this, in a locked drawer this office refuses to open, sits the actual cause: that I keep betting games I have no read on, chasing rehab-start unders and laying juice on every favorite that moves, the same way I always have. But that drawer stays closed. The injury report is right there, public, exhaustive, and so much easier to blame. If you would like to keep filing grievances with the rest of us, the whole Fuck All wing is accepting complaints, you can wallow properly down in Gaped, or limp through the slate with the lifers over in Degeneracy. The injured list never stops growing. Neither, apparently, does mine.
