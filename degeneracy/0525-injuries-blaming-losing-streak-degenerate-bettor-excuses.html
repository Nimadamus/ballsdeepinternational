Injuries I'm Blaming for My Losing Streak (A Comprehensive List)

May 25, 2026

I have lost eleven bets in a row. Eleven. That's not a slump, that's a curriculum. And after careful, scientific, two-beers-and-a-spreadsheet analysis, I have determined that not a single one of these losses was my fault. None of them. Zero personal responsibility detected. Instead, I have compiled a comprehensive, fully audited list of the injuries, ailments, holdouts, and miscellaneous human frailties that conspired to drain my checking account. If you are also on a losing streak, please feel free to copy my homework. It will not help, but it will feel incredible.

Let the record show that I am a victim here. I did nothing wrong except wager rent money on grown men whose bodies betrayed both them and, more importantly, me.

Exhibit A: Cale Makar's Upper Body, Wherever That Went

Let's start in the Western Conference Final, where the Vegas Golden Knights have built a 2-0 lead over the Colorado Avalanche, a team that won the Presidents' Trophy this year with the most regular season points in the league. I had the Avalanche. Of course I had the Avalanche. They were the best team in hockey for six straight months and I am not a moron, I am a man who watches standings.

Then Cale Makar, the best defenseman alive, missed the first two games of the series with an "upper-body injury." Upper body. That covers, what, everything from the belt up? That's a shoulder, a rib, a collarbone, a neck, or a sad feeling in his chest. The vagueness alone cost me money. I cannot bet confidently against a man whose injury is described with the specificity of a horoscope. Makar's upper body owes me $200 and I would like it located and served papers.

Exhibit B: Nathan MacKinnon Doing Everything Alone Like a Maniac

Still in Colorado, still bleeding money. With Makar out, Nathan MacKinnon has been dragging the Avalanche up the hill by himself, leading the team in playoff scoring like a one-man rescue mission. And here's the thing about a guy carrying an entire franchise on his back: it is beautiful, it is heroic, and it is absolutely useless to me when the other guys keep scoring more. MacKinnon, I love you, you are a warrior, and you have personally helped Mitch Marner lead the entire NHL postseason in points while my parlay quietly died in a Vegas back alley. Thanks for the effort. The effort does not cash.

Exhibit C: Lane Hutson Getting Folded in Half and Then Being Totally Fine

Over in the Eastern Conference Final, the Montreal Canadiens and Carolina Hurricanes are tied 1-1, and in Game 2 overtime, Lane Hutson got absolutely launched by a Taylor Hall hit along the boards. His leg got caught, the whole building gasped, and I, sitting on a Canadiens series bet, felt my soul leave my body. I had already started mentally drafting the excuse. "My guy got annihilated, what could I do."

And then the coach announced Hutson was fine. Fine! Completely fine. Playing in Game 3 at the Bell Centre fine. So now I don't even get the dignity of a real injury excuse. I just get the trauma of watching it happen with money on the line and then nothing changes. That's the worst of both worlds. Give me the injury or give me the win, do not give me a heart attack and a coin flip.

Reminder to self: a player being "fine after a scary hit" is great for the player, his family, and the sport of hockey, and catastrophic for a man who already spent the imaginary winnings on a used jet ski.

Exhibit D: Victor Wembanyama Being Eight Feet Tall and Furious

The NBA Western Conference Finals, Thunder versus Spurs, knotted up 2-2 heading into Game 5. I bet the defending champion Thunder because betting against the champs felt sharp and responsible. Then Victor Wembanyama decided to average roughly 29 points and 13 rebounds for the series, including a 41-point, 24-rebound double-overtime opener that looked like a man playing against middle schoolers. He dropped 33 in Game 4 to even it back up.

This is not an injury, technically, but I am adding it to the injury list because watching Wembanyama dunk on my moneyline gave ME the injury. Specifically a stress headache and a clenched jaw. I am filing it under workplace hazard. The Frenchman is healthy as an ox and that is precisely the problem. I was counting on someone being hurt and instead everyone on the Spurs is thriving spitefully.

Exhibit E: Jacoby Brissett Refusing to Work for $4.9 Million

Now we cross over into the NFL offseason, where there are no games, no scores, and yet I have still found a way to lose money on prop markets and futures. The Arizona Cardinals and quarterback Jacoby Brissett are "significantly" far apart on a reworked contract, and Brissett has skipped the entire voluntary offseason program because his base salary this year is a measly $4.9 million. He is holding out. Mid-May. In a sport that is not being played.

I had a flier on Arizona's win total going up, built entirely on the vibe that their guy looked good last season throwing for over 3,300 yards. Then the guy went 1-11 as a starter, the team finished 3-14, and now he won't even show up to throw to rookies in shorts. The man is staging a labor dispute over the offseason and somehow I am the one losing money. Jacoby, I respect the hustle, but your hold-out is holding my bankroll hostage.

Exhibit F: Malik Willis Signing With the Wrong Roster on Purpose

And finally, the Miami Dolphins. They moved on from Tua Tagovailoa, ate a mountain of dead money, and signed Malik Willis away from Green Bay on a three-year, $67.5 million deal to run the show. Then they turned around and shipped off Jaylen Waddle, so now my new quarterback is throwing to a receiving corps I have to look up. I had a longshot Dolphins ticket from when this all looked like a clever reunion with familiar coaches. It might still work out. It will not work out for me, because nothing does, but it might work out in the abstract for people who do not bet.

That is the full ledger. Six exhibits, eleven losses, and a single unifying theory of the universe: I am a brilliant handicapper, and the only variable I failed to account for is the entire human body and the free will of professional athletes. If Makar's shoulder, Hutson's leg, Wembanyama's everything, Brissett's contract, and Willis's new zip code had simply cooperated, I would be rich. Instead I am writing this. See you for the next slate, where I will discover a fresh batch of injuries to blame and absolutely no new self-awareness.
