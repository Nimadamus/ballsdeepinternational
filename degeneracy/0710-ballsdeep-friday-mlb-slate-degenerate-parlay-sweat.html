I Turned Tonight's Fifteen-Game Friday Slate Into an Eight-Team All-Favorites Parlay Because Chalk Feels Like Safety and Safety Is a Lie

July 10, 2026 | DEGENERACY WARD, FRIDAY NIGHT CHASE DIVISION

It is Friday, there are fifteen baseball games on the board, and I have had the kind of week that ends with a man doing math in the dark. Monday took a bite. Tuesday took a bigger one. By Thursday my bankroll looked like a phone charger cable, frayed at both ends and technically still functioning. And a degenerate does not respond to a losing week the way a healthy person does, which is by closing the app and going for a walk. A degenerate responds by looking at fifteen games at once and deciding that the safest possible move, the responsible move, the grown-up move, is to weld eight favorites into one parlay and call the whole thing a recovery plan.

The word I keep telling myself tonight is chalk, and chalk is the most dangerous word in my entire vocabulary, more dangerous than lock, more dangerous than sure thing, because chalk sounds like caution. It does not sound like gambling. It sounds like a man laying down heavy favorites the way a nervous investor buys index funds. That is the lie, and I know it is a lie, and I have taped the lie to my own forehead anyway. Laying a big price on a good team does not remove the risk. It just hides it inside a number ugly enough that you have to squint.

The Anchor Is a Number I Cannot Look At Directly

The centerpiece of my beautiful disaster is Shohei Ohtani taking the mound for the Dodgers against Arizona and Eduardo Rodriguez, with Los Angeles sitting somewhere around minus two sixty-five. Two sixty-five. To win a hundred dollars off that game by itself I would have to put down a number that makes my stomach do a small pirouette. So of course I did not bet it by itself. I did the degenerate thing and used the scariest number on the board as the load-bearing wall of a parlay, telling myself that Ohtani on the bump is not a bet, he is a foregone conclusion, he is the closest thing baseball has to a printing press.

Here is the part my brain refuses to hold onto for longer than four seconds. A minus two sixty-five favorite is not a coin that lands heads. It is a team the market thinks wins most of the time, and most of the time is a phrase that has personally destroyed me more than any underdog ever has. Ohtani can throw six shutout innings and strike out the side twice and the bullpen behind him can still hand it back in a way that makes me question whether God watches baseball. But tonight, on a Friday, chasing a bad week, that number does not read as a risk to me. It reads as an anchor. It reads as the safe leg. That is exactly how the anchor drowns you.

Then I Started Feeding the Rest of the Slate to the Machine

One favorite is a bet. Eight favorites is a lifestyle choice. So I went shopping down the fifteen-game slate the way a man with no self-control shops at a warehouse store, throwing things in the cart because they were technically on sale. Chris Sale toeing the rubber for the Braves over in St. Louis, and my brain went, well, that is a veteran ace, that is basically a free square, add it. Some other heavy home side pitching a guy with a shiny number next to his name, add it. A road favorite I would normally never touch, but the price felt fair and my judgment had already left the building, add it. Eight legs. Eight little certainties, none of them certain, all of them stapled together into one slip that pays like a jackpot and lives like a soap bubble.

Eight favorites is not eight ways to win. It is one way to win and eight separate trapdoors, and I built it on the night I could least afford to fall through any of them.

And because I have a sickness with a specific flavor, I could not stop myself from glancing at the Rockies. Colorado, thirty-eight and fifty-seven, a franchise that on the road turns into a rumor of a baseball team, the reliable national punchline of every full slate. There is a version of tonight where I fade them into my parlay as the easiest leg on the board, and there is another version where the baseball gods, who read this blog specifically to hurt me, decide that tonight is the night the worst road team in the sport wakes up and cashes for everyone in America except the man who left them off his slip. I have been on both sides of that specific knife. It never stops being sharp.

The Chase Is the Whole Disease

Let me be honest about what this parlay actually is, underneath the chalk and the confidence and the fake spreadsheet in my head. It is a chase. It is a man down for the week reaching for one big rounded number that erases four days of damage in a single Friday night, so he never has to sit with the losses, never has to feel the slow adult version of getting even. The all-favorites parlay is the chaser's drink of choice because it looks conservative and pays like a lottery ticket, which means it lets you feel responsible and greedy at the same time, and those two feelings holding hands is precisely how I got here.

A sane person recovers from a bad week by betting less, or not at all. I am recovering from a bad week by putting eight games in one basket and carrying the basket across a tightrope in the dark. If even one of those favorites decides, purely to spite me, that tonight is the night it lays an egg, the entire ticket turns to confetti and my week gets worse instead of whole. Eight legs means eight separate strangers all need to have a good night for me to have any night at all. If you have ever done this exact thing to yourself on a Friday, our whole Degeneracy ward keeps the lights on for you.

What I Learned, Which Is Nothing, Again

I know how this is supposed to go. You do not fix a losing week with a bigger swing, you fix it by putting the phone down and letting the wound scab over. Chalk is not safety. A heavy favorite is not a foregone conclusion, it is just a loss that costs more when it happens. And an eight-team parlay built on a Friday to erase a bad Monday is not a strategy, it is a confession with a payout attached. I have known all of this for years. It is stitched into me. It will not change one single thing about what I do the second first pitch gets close.

So tonight I will be on the couch with fifteen box scores open and one slip that needs all eight legs to survive, praying to Shohei Ohtani, to a Braves veteran in St. Louis, and to a handful of favorites whose names I will be screaming at a ceiling by the seventh inning. If it goes bad, and the math says it usually does, you can find the rest of us pretending to be fine over in Fuck All, or you can come all the way down to the bottom of the well with the true survivors in Gaped. The slate never runs out of games. The chase never runs out of Fridays. And I never, ever run out of reasons.
