The NBA Offseason Has No Games and I Am Somehow Still Losing Money

There is no basketball, there are no games, the season ended weeks ago, and the futures board has found a brand new way to take my rent

The NBA Offseason Has No Games and I Am Somehow Still Losing Money

June 15, 2026 | DEGENERACY WARD, OFFSEASON ISOLATION WING

The NBA Finals are over. The trophy got handed out. The confetti is swept up, the players are on yachts, and the entire basketball world has agreed to take a few months off and touch grass. And yet here I am, at my desk, at an hour no functioning adult should be awake, staring at a betting board that has zero basketball games on it and somehow finding a way to lose money on it anyway. This is the part of the calendar nobody warns you about. The offseason is not a break. It is a trap with no clock on it.

Here is the thing about a real degenerate. A normal fan sees an empty schedule and feels relief, goes outside, calls their mother, remembers they have a body. I see an empty schedule and feel a cold panic, like a shark that has stopped swimming. No games means no action, and no action means I have to sit alone with my thoughts, and my thoughts are mostly just bet slips I have not placed yet. So I do the only logical thing. I find the markets that never close, and the books, bless their predatory hearts, have built an entire casino out of nothing happening at all.

Championship Futures: Betting On Games That Do Not Exist Yet

The first door in the offseason casino is the championship futures board, and it is a beautiful con. Next season has not started. Rosters are not set. Half these guys do not know what city they will be playing in. And I am over here laying real money on a team to win a title in a season that is, conservatively, four months from tipping off. I am not betting on a game. I am betting on a vibe, a rumor, and a depth chart that exists only in a beat reporter's group chat.

The genius of the futures bet is that it locks your money up for half a year and then makes you watch every single transaction like it is a referee's call. A team I bet on signs a guy, I feel like a genius for nine minutes. The same team's other guy gets hurt, and suddenly my "lock" is dead money I cannot even cash out without eating a loss. I have turned the entire summer into a slow-motion bad beat where the buzzer does not sound until December. Who designed this. Why did I walk in. Why am I nodding like it makes sense.

The Draft Is Just Prop Bets For People Who Have Given Up

Then there is the draft, which the offseason degenerate treats like the Super Bowl of having no idea what is going on. Who goes first overall. Who goes in the top five. Will this prospect get drafted higher or lower than some number a book invented specifically to confuse me. I do not scout. I have never watched a full college game in my life that I did not have money on. But I will absolutely fire a unit on a guy's draft position based on a mock draft I skimmed for eleven seconds, because the alternative is admitting I have nothing to bet, and that is not a feeling I am equipped to survive.

The draft props are pure chaos and that is exactly why my brain lights up. There is no skill here, no edge, no homework that helps, just a roulette wheel dressed up in a suit and a green room. And the second the picks start, I am locked in, sweating the order of names like my mortgage depends on which teenager shakes the commissioner's hand third. A normal person watches the draft for the hope and the highlights. I watch it because I made it financially personal for absolutely no reason.

I Bet On The Coaching Carousel, Which Is Just Betting On Office Politics

And then, in the deepest, dampest corner of the offseason casino, I found it. The coaching carousel market. Odds on which team fires its coach next. Odds on who gets the open job. I have officially descended to the point where I am wagering on the human resources decisions of basketball franchises. This is not sports betting. This is fantasy office politics, and I am playing it with money I do not have, sweating a press conference like it is overtime.

Do you understand how far gone you have to be to have a rooting interest in a coaching search. I do not know these executives. I do not know their process. But I put forty dollars on a hire and now I am refreshing a reporter's feed at 2 AM hoping a franchise I do not support makes a personnel move I have no insight into, so that a ticket I should never have bought can cash. The offseason did this to me. The offseason takes a man with no games to bet and convinces him to bet on the games behind the games.

What I Learned, Which Is, As Always, Nothing

The lesson here is simple and I will not be applying it. The offseason is not a betting opportunity. It is a quiet, gameless stretch of months specifically designed by the universe to let a degenerate rest, heal, and remember what a weekend feels like without a sweat. The futures are slow, the draft props are a coin flip, and the coaching odds are an invitation to lose money on a sport that is not even being played. The correct move is to close the app and go live a life.

Instead, I have a championship future tying up my bankroll until winter, three draft-position props I cannot defend, and a live ticket riding on whether a team I do not care about hires a coach I have never met. There are no games. There is no action. And I am still, somehow, in the red. Welcome to the offseason isolation wing, where the schedule is empty, the markets never sleep, and a man can go broke betting on absolutely nothing at all.
