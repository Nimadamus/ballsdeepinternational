Injuries I'm Blaming for My Losing Streak: A Comprehensive List of Sports Betting Excuses

A degenerate gambler's fully itemized accountability dodge across the NBA, NHL, and NFL

May 24, 2026

I want to be very clear about something before we begin: my losing streak is not my fault. I have reviewed the tape. I have consulted my spreadsheet, which is now long enough to qualify as a historical document. And after careful, sober analysis conducted entirely at 2 AM while eating cold lo mein over a sink, I have concluded that every single bad beat I have suffered traces back to an injury report, a healthy scratch, a phantom hamstring, or a contract dispute that has nothing to do with me but somehow targeted me personally.

This is the list. This is the comprehensive, itemized, fully cross-referenced list of injuries I'm blaming for my losing streak. If you've ever stared at a sportsbook app and whispered "it wasn't supposed to go like this," welcome to the Degeneracy ward. You're among family here.

This is roughly the energy of me reading an injury report at 1 AM and deciding it ruined my life.

Exhibit A: Wembanyama and the Western Conference Finals That Personally Targeted Me

Victor Wembanyama is on the Spurs. The Spurs are down 2-1 to the Thunder in the Western Conference Finals. Now, you'd think a seven-foot-five alien who blocks shots from the next zip code would be enough to carry a futures ticket. I certainly thought so, because I put real American currency on San Antonio with the confidence of a man who has never once been right about anything.

And it's 2-1 Thunder. TWO TO ONE. Not a sweep, not a blowout, just close enough to torture me. Every Spurs possession that doesn't end in a Wembanyama dunk feels like a personal insult. I'm not blaming Wemby exactly, I'm blaming the universe for putting a generational talent on a team that's down a game with my money attached. The man is averaging numbers that don't make sense on a calculator and I still found a way to be sweating. That's a me problem disguised as a basketball problem, which is the only kind of problem I have.

Exhibit B: The Knicks, the Cavaliers, and the Coin Flip I Lost on Purpose

Knicks at Cavaliers. A perfectly normal NBA game that I turned into a referendum on my self-worth. I had a side. I will not tell you which side, because both sides have hurt me before and I refuse to give either franchise the satisfaction. What I will tell you is that somewhere in the fourth quarter, a role player I have never heard of did something heroic, and my ticket went from "alive and breathing" to "cause of death: confidence."

Is this an injury? Not technically. But I'm classifying it as a self-inflicted one, because nobody forced me to bet a road favorite on the second night of a back-to-back except the little gremlin in my brain who keeps saying "this one feels different." It never feels different. It always feels exactly the same. The gremlin is undefeated and I am 4-19 this month.

Exhibit C: The Avalanche and the Golden Knights Missed-Opportunity Special

Hockey. My nemesis. My beautiful, incomprehensible nemesis. I had the Avalanche, and Colorado spent an entire game lamenting missed opportunities against the Golden Knights like a man who left his wallet in a cab. They had chances. They had so many chances. The puck did everything except go in the net, and every clanged post was a tiny hammer tapping directly on my will to live.

I do not understand hockey. I have admitted this in our Fuck All section before and I'll admit it again. I bet on hockey the way a raccoon operates a vending machine: with great enthusiasm and zero comprehension. But the Avalanche's missed opportunities are now officially on my list of injuries, because watching a team fail to capitalize is its own kind of trauma, and trauma is an injury of the soul, and the soul is part of the body, so legally this counts.

Exhibit D: Jacoby Brissett, the Cardinals, and a Contract That Won't Close

You're going to laugh, but I had an NFL futures angle that hinged, loosely, in the dumbest way possible, on Arizona's quarterback situation feeling settled. Then the news drops: Jacoby Brissett is not close to a reworked deal with the Cardinals. Not close! The man and the franchise are sitting at opposite ends of a long table not signing anything, and somehow my offseason futures ticket is sweating because of it.

Is Jacoby Brissett injured? No. Is Jacoby Brissett's contract injured? Absolutely. It's in a full body cast. And in the degenerate's medical handbook, a stalled contract negotiation is a Grade 2 strain of my financial planning. I built a tiny house of cards on the assumption that NFL offseason stability would help a number I bet on in a moment of weakness, and Jacoby Brissett walked past my house of cards and sneezed.

Running tally of things I am blaming instead of myself: one alien on the Spurs, one anonymous Knicks-Cavs hero, every post the Avalanche hit, and one quarterback's unsigned contract. Notably absent from this list: me, my decisions, and the eight-leg parlay I built before brushing my teeth.

Exhibit E: The Phantom Injuries I Invented Entirely in My Own Head

This is the deep cut. These are the injuries that don't appear on any official report because I made them up to protect myself emotionally. The point guard who "looked a little tired in warmups." The pitcher who "seemed off in the bullpen, I could tell." The backup center who "definitely tweaked something" in a way that conveniently explains why my under died by 35 points. None of these are real. All of these are load-bearing pillars of my self-esteem.

I have become a forensic injury analyst with zero medical training and a profound financial incentive to find a culprit that isn't the man in the mirror. I watch a guy jog back on defense and I think "that's a 70% hamstring, book it." I'm not a doctor. I'm a guy with a betting app and a grudge against reality.

The Diagnosis: It Was Never the Injuries

Here's the part where, if I were a healthier person, I'd admit that injuries happen to both teams, that variance is real, that no single hamstring is responsible for a streak this long and this stupid. The Spurs being down to the Thunder didn't drain my account. The Avalanche hitting posts didn't open my sportsbook app and add a fourth leg. Jacoby Brissett's contract is, somehow, genuinely none of my business.

But I am not a healthier person. I am a degenerate, and the entire load-bearing structure of degeneracy is the unshakable belief that the bet was right and the world was wrong. So I'm filing all of these under "injuries," I'm closing the spreadsheet, and I'm building tomorrow's slate with the unearned confidence of a man who learned nothing.
