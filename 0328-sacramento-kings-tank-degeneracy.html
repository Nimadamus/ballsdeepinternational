A Degenerate's Saturday Diary: Sacramento Kings Edition, or How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Parlay the Tank

March 28, 2026 • 6 min read • Written under the influence of bad decisions

It's 9:47 AM on a Saturday and I'm already looking at the Sacramento Kings' record like it's a cry for help. 19-55. Nineteen wins. The Kings have won nineteen professional basketball games this season and I'm sitting here, coffee in one hand, phone in the other, genuinely considering taking them on the moneyline tonight against the Hawks. Why? Because I'm a degenerate, and degenerates don't ask why. Degenerates ask "what's the payout?"

Look at this slate. Really look at it. The NBA handed us a Saturday card that looks like it was designed in a laboratory specifically to destroy gambling addicts. Sacramento at Atlanta. Utah at Phoenix. Detroit at Minnesota. And the crown jewel, the absolute masterpiece of unwatchable basketball: Chicago Bulls (29-44) versus the Memphis Grizzlies (24-49). That's a combined 53-93 record. That's not a basketball game, that's a support group.

7:00 AM: The Morning Parlay (Mistake #1)

I woke up, checked my phone, and immediately started building a parlay. This is not healthy behavior. Normal people wake up, stretch, maybe eat some eggs. I wake up and start cross-referencing the Kings' road record with their third-quarter scoring averages because some guy on Twitter said the under is "free money." Nothing is free money. I've been doing this for years and nothing has ever been free. But here I am, adding legs to this parlay like I'm assembling furniture from IKEA with no instructions and full confidence.

The Morning Parlay of Doom
Hawks ML + Suns ML + Timberwolves ML + Grizzlies ML + 4 NHL favorites

Eight legs. I put eight legs on this thing before I brushed my teeth. The payout looks beautiful on paper and that's exactly the problem. It always looks beautiful on paper. You know what else looked beautiful on paper? The Titanic blueprints.

10:30 AM: The Kings Research Spiral

I have now spent 45 minutes reading about the Sacramento Kings like they're going to tell me something I don't already know. They're 19-55. They are historically, cosmically, generationally terrible. They're not tanking, they're already at the bottom of the ocean. They passed through tanking three months ago. They're in whatever comes after tanking. Decomposition.

And yet. AND YET. The Hawks are only 41-33, and they've been sloppy on defense lately, and maybe, just maybe, the Kings catch them sleeping on a Saturday afternoon and I... no. Stop. This is exactly how they get you. You start rationalizing a Kings bet and next thing you know you're in a Denny's parking lot at 2 AM trying to figure out how to deposit more money.

12:00 PM: The Tank Bowl, Baby

Bulls versus Grizzlies might be the most honest basketball game of the season. Neither team is pretending anymore. The Bulls are 29-44 and playing with the intensity of a group project where everyone knows they're getting a C regardless. Memphis is 24-49 and has fully embraced the void. This isn't basketball. This is two teams slow-dancing toward the lottery while occasionally putting a ball through a hoop.

Naturally, I'm betting the over. Because when two terrible teams play each other, the game somehow always turns into an accidental offensive showcase. Nobody plays defense. Nobody boxes out. Rebounds bounce around like pinballs. Someone scores 35 points and you check their season average and it's 11. The Tank Bowl doesn't follow the laws of basketball. The Tank Bowl follows the laws of chaos.

"You know what the real tank is? My bankroll. My bankroll is 19-55." - Me, to myself, in the mirror, at noon on a Saturday.

2:00 PM: NHL Enters the Chat

Ten NHL games tonight. Ten. The NHL said "here, have a full plate of chaos on a Saturday" and I responded by immediately adding four hockey legs to an already cursed parlay. I know nothing about hockey. I watch hockey the way a golden retriever watches a ceiling fan. I'm mesmerized, I'm engaged, I have absolutely no idea what's happening, and I'm having the time of my life.

But sure, let me parlay four NHL moneylines with the confidence of a man who definitely knows what icing is. (I do not know what icing is. I've been told multiple times. It does not stick.) Hockey is the sport where your parlay dies in the third period because some guy named Jarkko scores a power play goal with 47 seconds left. Every single time. Jarkko is my nemesis and he doesn't even know I exist.

3:00 PM: MLB Opening Week, Oh God

Baseball is back, which means I now have ANOTHER sport to lose money on. Opening week MLB is the purest form of degeneracy because you're betting on sample sizes that don't exist yet. Who's pitching well? Nobody knows, it's been four games. What's the bullpen situation? Complete mystery. Is this team's lineup going to mash? Literally no human on earth can tell you.

So obviously I'm hammering run lines and first five innings and player props like I have insider information from the future. I don't have insider information from the future. I have a phone, a depleted bank account, and an unwavering belief that this time will be different.

5:00 PM: The Pre-Game Existential Crisis

Games start soon and I've got action on everything. NBA, NHL, MLB. I'm spread thinner than cream cheese on a conference room bagel. My parlay has so many legs it looks like a centipede. If everything hits, I'll make enough money to feel like a genius for approximately 12 hours before I put it all back on Sunday's slate.

This is the cycle. This is always the cycle. Win, feel invincible, overbet, lose, feel terrible, chase, lose more, tell yourself "I'm done," wake up the next morning and build another eight-leg parlay before brushing your teeth. The Sacramento Kings are 19-55 and somehow they're in better shape than my gambling habits. At least they know they're rebuilding. I'm just deteriorating.

If you or someone you know is betting on the Sacramento Kings road moneyline in March, that's not a gambling problem. That's performance art. Call 1-800-GAMBLER or just sit with the pain like the rest of us.

See you tomorrow when I do it all again. Go Kings.
